# 01 — References and Citations

Every reference cited in the source article (the viviennaBTC X.com post + the longer SDF-framework essay) was checked against primary sources. None are fabricated. This file documents what each paper actually says and how strongly it supports the article's claims.

## Cited in the article (5)

### Ang, A. (2014). *Asset Management: A Systematic Approach to Factor Investing*. Oxford UP.

- **ISBN:** 978-0199959327
- **Status:** ✅ Real book, in print since 2014
- **Chapter cited:** Ch. 11, *"Real" Assets*
- **Key findings the article draws on:**
  - Correlation of monthly gold returns with US CPI: **0.01** (Jan 1986 – Dec 2011)
  - Correlation of monthly precious-metal returns with US CPI: **0.02** (same period)
  - Long-horizon annual gold-CPI correlation: **0.23** (Sept 1875 – Dec 2011)
  - Among commodities, only **energy** is a "decent" inflation hedge (correlation 0.26 same period)
  - Real estate (REITs) inflation correlation is **0.40 at five-year horizons** but **-0.10 at <1-year horizons**
- **Where the article extrapolates beyond Ang:**
  - The article cites the gold-CPI correlation as **0.08**. This is not Ang's specific 0.01 number; it's in the ballpark but uses an unstated longer window (likely 1975-2012).
  - The three-regime taxonomy (low-inflation / moderate-trap / unanchored) is **the article's own synthesis**, not in Ang's text.
- **Links:** [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/3342) · [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/commodity-futures/real-assets-and-inflation/)

### Cochrane, J. H. (2005). *Asset Pricing* (Revised Edition). Princeton UP.

- **Status:** ✅ Standard graduate textbook
- **Key findings the article draws on:**
  - The fundamental asset-pricing equation `1 = E[M·R]`
  - The covariance form of risk premia: `E[R] − Rf = −Rf · Cov(M, R)`
  - The interpretation of M as a "bad times" pricing kernel
- **Where the article extrapolates:**
  - The article's claim that the real interest rate is a "real-time temperature reading on M" is broadly consistent with consumption-based SDF derivations in Cochrane Ch. 1-2 but is not Cochrane's exact framing.
- **Verdict:** Used appropriately as textbook foundation.

### Ang, A., Hodrick, R., Xing, Y., & Zhang, X. (2006). "The cross-section of volatility and expected returns." *Journal of Finance* 61(1), 259-299.

- **Status:** ✅ Real paper, foundational result in equity factor literature
- **Topic:** Idiosyncratic volatility pricing in the cross-section of US stocks
- **Verdict:** **Tangential to the gold thesis.** Cited as supporting factor-investing background but does not directly support any gold-specific claim in the article.

### Erb, C. B., & Harvey, C. R. (2013). "The Golden Dilemma." *Financial Analysts Journal* 69(4), 10-42. NBER w18706.

- **Status:** ✅ Real paper, one of the most-cited works on gold pricing
- **Key findings the article draws on (and some it omits):**
  - **Drawn on:** Gold is a poor short-term inflation hedge — gold-CPI relationship is driven by a single year (1980)
  - **Drawn on:** Strong negative gold ↔ real-rate correlation — they report **-0.82** between gold price level and the real rate
  - **Drawn on:** Gold's inflation-hedge property only manifests at "century-scale" horizons, not investment horizons
  - **OMITTED by the article:** Their "real price of gold mean reverts" finding — when the inflation-adjusted gold price is historically high, subsequent real gold returns have been below average. **This is currently relevant: we are near historical real-price highs.**
  - **OMITTED:** Their explicit warning that the "real price of gold may rise even further from today's elevated levels" if EM central banks accumulate — *which is exactly what happened post-2022.* The article does not credit Erb-Harvey for predicting the dynamic.
- **Links:** [NBER w18706](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18706) · [SSRN 2078535](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2078535)

### Baur, D. G., & Lucey, B. M. (2010). "Is Gold a Hedge or a Safe Haven? An Analysis of Stocks, Bonds and Gold." *Financial Review* 45(2), 217-229.

- **Status:** ✅ Real paper, foundational hedge/safe-haven distinction
- **Key findings the article draws on (and some it stretches):**
  - **Drawn on:** Gold is a hedge against stocks "on average" (positive returns when stocks fall, average)
  - **Drawn on:** Gold is a safe haven in extreme stock-market conditions
  - **OMITTED:** **The safe-haven property is short-lived — about 15 trading days.** The article treats the B (tail-hedge) position as a permanent strategic hold, which is a stretch from Baur-Lucey's actual finding.
  - Markets studied: US, UK, Germany. Time period: 1995-2005.
- **Verdict:** Used to ground the A/B position split conceptually. The 15-trading-day duration finding should be surfaced in the dashboard's "B position" copy.
- **Links:** [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6288.2010.00244.x) · [SSRN 952289](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=952289)

## Additional sources used in cross-validation (not cited by the article)

These were consulted to test the article's claims and to fill the gaps (especially the post-2022 break).

### Caldara, D., & Iacoviello, M. (2022). "Measuring Geopolitical Risk." *American Economic Review* 112(4), 1194-1225.

- **Status:** ✅ Peer-reviewed; authors are Fed economists
- **Provides:** The GPR (Geopolitical Risk) index — both daily (GPRD) and monthly (GPR) series — used as viviennaBTC's F5
- **Index methodology:** Text-search of 10 major newspapers, share of articles mentioning adverse geopolitical events
- **Coverage:** 1985-present (benchmark); GPRH historical back to 1900
- **Links:** [Fed IFDP 1222](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/files/ifdp1222.pdf) · [policyuncertainty.com](https://www.policyuncertainty.com/gpr.html) · [matteoiacoviello.com data download](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/gpr.htm)

### RBC Wealth Management (2024-2025). "Gold's regime change."

- **Status:** ✅ Industry research; corroborated by S&P Global, PIMCO, World Gold Council
- **Headline finding:** Gold ↔ 10Y real-yield correlation collapsed from **-84% (2005-2021)** to **-3% (2022-2023)** to **-7% (2024-present)**
- **Link:** [rbcwealthmanagement.com](https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-us/insights/golds-regime-change)

### World Gold Council (2025). "Gold Demand Trends Q4 and Full Year 2025."

- **Status:** ✅ Industry-standard quarterly demand series
- **Key findings:**
  - Central bank net purchases: **863 tonnes in 2025**, down from >1,000t in 2022/2023/2024
  - Survey: **95% of central banks** expect global official gold reserves to rise in next 12 months (highest in 8-year survey history)
  - **43% of CBs** indicate plans to increase their own holdings (record)
- **Link:** [gold.org/goldhub Q4 2025](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-full-year-2025)

### LBMA Alchemist Issue 90 (2018). "An Update on Gold, Real Interest Rates and the Dollar."

- **Status:** ✅ Industry research from LBMA
- **Regression result:** Weekly 2014-2018, **R² = 0.65**, **-$173/oz per 1pp rise in 10Y TIPS yield over 13 weeks**
- **Caveat:** Pre-2022, before the regime break
- **Link:** [lbma.org.uk](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-90/an-update-on-gold-real-interest-rates-and-the-dollar)

### Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago (2021). "What Drives Gold Prices?" Chicago Fed Letter 464.

- **Status:** ✅ Fed staff research
- **Link:** [chicagofed.org](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/chicago-fed-letter/2021/464)

### PIMCO (n.d.). "Understanding Gold Prices."

- **Status:** ✅ Industry research
- **Finding the article aligns with:** "Changes in real (inflation-adjusted) yields have been the most significant drivers of gold prices over the past couple of decades" — **but PIMCO themselves note this relationship weakened post-2022.**
- **Link:** [pimco.com](https://www.pimco.com/us/en/resources/education/understanding-gold-prices)
