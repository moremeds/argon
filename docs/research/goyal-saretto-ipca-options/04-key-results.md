## Table 4 — RV−IV decile decomposition (p.1804)

The table decomposes each of the 10 RV−IV deciles into: realized return Ret, IPCA expected return E(Ret), IPCA α, the three factor-beta-contribution terms β_{F1}E(F1), β_{F2}E(F2), β_{F3}E(F3), and the RV−IV characteristic value itself.

Key visible pattern: monotonically increasing across deciles for both `Ret` and `E(Ret)`. The IPCA model lines up *very* well on the cross-section (only decile 1 has a noticeable α gap). β_{F1}E(F1) varies from −2.63 (decile 1) to +1.83 (decile 10), an enormous spread — confirming that almost all the cross-sectional spread comes from F1 loadings, and F1 loadings come from spread in the RV−IV characteristic itself.

## The "RV−IV centrality" findings

Two distinct experiments make the same point.

### Γ_β zero-out (§6 Conclusion, p.1815)

> "Setting the covariance coefficients, Γ_β, related to RV−IV to zero greatly decreases the ability of the IPCA model to price any portfolio that is not related to RV−IV: for example, 32 strategies see an increase in alpha and that increase is 1% on average."

### Factor-return decomposition (§5.3.1, Figure 4)

The three IPCA factor returns can be written as linear combinations of the 47 managed-portfolio returns (one per characteristic):
- F1 (avg ret 2.06%/mo, ann. SR 5.1): contribution dominated by the **RV−IV** managed portfolio (largest single bar, ≈ +0.95).
- F2 (avg ret 0.26%/mo, ann. SR ≈ low): closely related to the **EW market option portfolio**, inversely loaded (largest bar in absolute value).
- F3 (avg ret 0.11%/mo, ann. SR ≈ low): a long-short of **RV−IV and Option price**.

### Connection to economic conditions (Table 7)

- F1 = lower-return states when market return is below median / VIX rises (avg diff −0.34, *t*=−2.10) / tail risk is high (−0.46, *t*=−2.80) / vol risk is high (+1.32, *t*=+8.68) / kurt risk is low (−0.81, *t*=−4.79).
- F2 and F3 mostly act as hedges against those same negative shocks.
- **Tangency portfolio Sharpe:** in-sample 5.8, out-of-sample 4.4 (gross); **net-of-TC 4.1 in-sample, 2.7 OOS** — economically large, but, again, on managed portfolios that include the bid-ask costs only at initiation.

## What does *not* hold

- Pre-2016 IPCA on the Zhan-et-al-10 characteristics in their **original** time window has a few alphas approach MHT significance (Table 6 IS rows). So the IPCA-alpha-explains-everything result *does* depend on the post-2016 sample being included. This is the closest the paper comes to admitting a sample-period sensitivity.
- The Wald *p*-value test for unrestricted-α is rejected at K ≤ 4 (p = .00–.03), meaning the constrained Γ_α = 0 model is statistically rejected — but the *economic* magnitudes of the implied alphas are tiny (~6bp on 80bp raw). The paper treats this as evidence that 3 factors are the *economically* preferred K, not a clean H0 acceptance.
