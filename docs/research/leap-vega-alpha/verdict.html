The "cheap LEAP" edge is not vega — it's a directional bet.

Tested radon's thesis: when realized vol (HV20/HV60) sits well above a long-dated option's implied vol, buying the LEAP is supposed to harvest forward-vol mispricing. On 6 months of banked surface data it doesn't. The signal is real, but the money in it is 82–88% delta — a leveraged long on high-vol names in an up-market. The isolated vega edge loses to theta before it ever reaches the spread.

Vol points, delta-hedged and net of theta. The vega you harvest (ΔIV +3.2 / +5.7 vp raw) is real, but a long LEAP bleeds ≈−3.8 / −7.0 vp of theta over the same hold, leaving 0.6–0.7 vp. You then cross a 1–5 vp round-trip spread twice. The edge never clears the tightest plausible cost.

How the two stages read

Stage 1 · signal

There is a real relationship

Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional IC 0.34 / 0.43 (20d / 40d, single-name). The mechanical mean-reversion null is rejected — IC(−IV) is −0.14, not positive — and HV alone forecasts forward IV (+0.25). Not noise, not an artifact.

Stage 2 · kill

…but it isn't paid as vega

Decompose the held-contract P&L and 82–88% is delta. The clean, hedged vega edge nets 0.6–0.7 vp after theta — under the spread. "IV moves" ≠ "you get paid." A market bet dressed as vol-alpha.

What keeps this honest

  • 1One regime, and a kind one. Dec 2025 → Jul 2026: prices and vol rose, inflating both the delta P&L and the raw vega harvest. A falling-vol tape would likely push even the 0.6–0.7 vp net negative.
  • 2Thin independent evidence. The strong t-stats (13–24) are autocorrelation-inflated; the non-overlapping sample is only 3–6 independent dates. The point estimates are positive; the significance is weak.
  • 3Units were measured, not assumed. Grid call_vega is per-1%-vol and call_theta per-day (the CLAUDE.md "vega ×100" note is wrong for this table) — checked against a real AAPL contract before trusting the split.
